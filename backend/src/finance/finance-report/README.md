# Financial Reporting Architecture & Accounting Standards

This document outlines the core architectural and accounting decisions that govern the financial reporting module (Profit & Loss and Balance Sheet) in the Samugara ERP. It serves as a single source of truth for developers and architects to understand *why* data is queried and structured in specific ways.

## 1. Core Philosophy: Flow vs. Snapshot

The financial reports are divided into two fundamental paradigms:

*   **Profit & Loss (Laba Rugi):** Represents **Flow (Aktivitas Mutasi)**. It measures the financial performance over a specific period (e.g., Jan 1 - Dec 31).
    *   **Lifespan & Reset:** The P&L has a lifespan of exactly one fiscal period. On December 31st, the Net Profit is finalized and moved to the Balance Sheet. On January 1st, the P&L is completely reset to zero (0). Revenue and Expenses do not carry over.
    *   **Data Source:** MUST source data from **mutational tables** (e.g., `FinancialTransaction`) filtered strictly by the target year.

*   **Balance Sheet (Neraca):** Represents a **Snapshot (Saldo Akhir)**. It measures the accumulated position of assets, liabilities, and equity at a specific point in time.
    *   **Lifespan (Cumulative):** The Balance Sheet is **cumulative from Day 1** of the company's existence. It is never reset. An asset balance on Dec 31st remains identical on Jan 1st.
    *   **Data Source:** SHOULD source data from **master/balance tables** (e.g., `AccountReceivable`, `AccountPayable`) to retrieve the accurate historical cumulative balance efficiently.

### The Missing Link: Retained Earnings
The P&L and Balance Sheet communicate via **Retained Earnings (Laba Ditahan)**. The Net Profit generated by the P&L each year does not disappear; it accumulates year over year inside the Retained Earnings equity account on the Balance Sheet.

## 2. Income Tax in Profit & Loss

In the P&L, `Income Tax` acts as a deduction to arrive at `Net Profit`.

### Why query from `FinancialTransaction`?
To accurately reflect the tax deductions that occurred *during the current year*, we must look at the actual transactional flow. Querying a master table would incorrectly yield a cumulative balance from previous years.

### The `Non Cash & Bank` (OTHER) Filter
When customers withhold PPh 23 from their payments, the withheld amount never enters the operational bank account. It is recorded as a Memorial/Adjustment journal entry. By filtering `internalAccount.type = 'OTHER'` and `holderName` containing `non cash & bank`, we successfully isolate these non-cash tax deduction journals, keeping them pure from operational cash flows.

### Why take the Debit Column (`colC`)?
Prepaid Taxes (like Bukti Potong PPh 23) are considered **Assets**. In accounting, an increase in an asset is recorded as a **Debit**. When a customer withholds PPh 23, our Prepaid Tax asset *increases*, meaning the value is captured in the Debit column (`colC`). 
*Note: In the code, we explicitly subtract (`minus`) this Debit value so that it correctly acts as a deduction against the Profit Before Tax.*

### The Explicit Exclusion of PPh 25 (Strict Compliance)
**PPh 25 (Monthly Corporate Tax Installments) MUST NOT reduce the monthly P&L Net Profit.** 
PPh 25 is merely a transfer of assets: from Cash (Bank) to Prepaid Tax (Asset). It is not an expense. It only offsets Corporate Tax (PPh 29) at year-end calculation.
To ensure the P&L is bulletproof and strictly compliant with enterprise accounting standards, `PPh 25` is intentionally **omitted** from the P&L Income Tax filters. This prevents any human data-entry errors from accidentally reducing the Net Profit.

## 3. Prepaid Tax in Balance Sheet

In the Balance Sheet, `Prepaid Tax` (PPh 21, 23, 25, PPN) is displayed under Current Assets.

### Why query from `AccountReceivable` (AR)?
1.  **Accounting Classification:** Prepaid Tax represents a "claim" or right to offset future tax liabilities with the government. Because its nature is identical to a receivable (Piutang), the ERP groups Prepaid Tax ledgers inside the AR module.
2.  **Performance (Zero Waste Logic):** The Balance Sheet requires the total outstanding balance accumulated over the company's lifespan. Calculating this by summing every transaction in `FinancialTransaction` since day one is highly inefficient. The `AccountReceivable` table (specifically `colR`) already maintains the active, cumulative outstanding balance. By querying the AR table, the system achieves a highly performant, deterministic, and accurate snapshot.
